## Reading the pattern

The jurisdictions whose residents generate more need than the 6th-cycle method would allocate to them (Unincorporated County, El Cajon, Oceanside, Chula Vista) are not the ones the allocation concentrates on (San Diego, National City, Escondido, Solana Beach). The allocation method is 65% transit stations and 35% jobs, so it concentrates units where rail, Rapid and frequent bus infrastructure already exists; the existing-need lines (overcrowding, cost burden, substandard vacancy) follow where lower-income renters actually live. A jurisdiction with a positive gap houses need that the allocation sends elsewhere; a negative gap means the method assigns it more than its own residents generate. That asymmetry is structural - it cannot be negotiated away inside the allocation methodology process, because the methodology's own factors (stations, jobs) are what produce it.
