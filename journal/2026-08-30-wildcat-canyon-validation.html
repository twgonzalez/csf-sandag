The Wildcat Canyon validation

The evacuation standard now has zones nobody drew, tested against fires nobody chose — and it found the road where the Cedar Fire killed.

Replaying the 2003 Cedar Fire as an evacuation, the model's binding chokepoint is Wildcat Canyon Road — where most of that fire's civilian deaths occurred.

The model was built from OpenStreetMap geometry, Census vehicle counts, and federal capacity defaults. It contains no fire-specific tuning and no knowledge of the fire's history. Its other Cedar corridors — Ashwood, Mapleview, San Vicente — are the Lakeside–Ramona roads of the historical record. This is now a permanent known-answer test: any change that stops surfacing Wildcat Canyon fails the build.

The standard, in one paragraph

An evacuation shed is the set of neighborhoods whose escape routes converge on the same bottleneck — watershed logic applied to roads. Nobody draws the sheds: they are implied by the public road network, measured vehicle ownership, and a stated routing rule, so there is no boundary an analyst chose and nothing discretionary to defend. Fire ignores city lines; so do sheds. Each tract's score is its shed's discharge rate — homes out per hour through the shared bottleneck — which is the number that decides outcomes when a fast fire allows an hour, not an afternoon.

Why it is defensible

  • "Who drew these zones?" Nobody — the road network implies them, from the same public inputs as everything else.
  • "Why these test scenarios?" They are fires that actually happened, from CAL FIRE's own perimeter database.
  • "Can I check it?" Every bottleneck's load, capacity, and contributing neighborhoods are published; a fire marshal can audit the routing on a map, and a resident can find their evacuation community by street name.

Stated limits stand: v1 capacities are uncalibrated federal defaults; the match is strongest for the large fires where stakes are highest and weakest for small rural burns; all clearance clocks are model quantities, never predictions. Details in reports/evacuation_sheds.md.
