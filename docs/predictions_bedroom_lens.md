# Predictions: the bedroom lens

Committed **before** the analysis is computed, per the standing rule. Motivation: RHNA counts
units, and a studio counts the same as a four-bedroom house. On expensive coastal land, the
units that pencil are small ones — so a unit-denominated coastal obligation may deliver far
fewer *bedrooms* (the thing families need near good schools) than the ledger suggests. This
analysis measures the existing landscape and prices that gap. Basis for the priors: general
knowledge of the region's building stock; no bedroom tabulations have been run.

Conventions: bedrooms per unit uses ACS B25041 with a studio counted as one sleeping room and
"5+" counted as 5 (the pipeline's existing convention, which *understates* large-home
differences — bias runs against prediction 1, not for it).

1. **The family-sized stock near good schools already exists.** High/Highest Resource tracts
   county-wide average at least 15% more bedrooms per unit than the county as a whole. The
   studio question is about what gets ADDED, not what stands.
2. **All five high-resource coastal cities beat the county average** bedrooms per unit in
   their existing stock (Carlsbad, Encinitas, Solana Beach, Del Mar, Coronado).
3. **The studio loophole is worth ≥ 20,000 bedrooms across the four coastal-archetype
   cities.** Meeting the fairness-baseline obligations of Carlsbad + Encinitas + Solana
   Beach + Del Mar at a small-unit mix (1.0 bedroom per unit) delivers no more than half the
   bedrooms of building at each tract's existing local mix, and the absolute gap across the
   four is at least 20,000 bedrooms.
4. **Coronado has the lowest bedrooms per unit of the five** (condo- and Navy-heavy stock) —
   the one high-resource coastal city where the small-unit equilibrium is already partly
   priced in.

Misses get published next to hits.
